# One-call conversions and stream adapters: cost per call, throughput, and size, 2026-11-03

What wave 4's conveniences cost over the streaming `Decoder` and `Encoder`
they wrap: `decodeToUtf8`, `decodeToUtf16`, `encodeFromUtf8`, and
`encodeFromUtf16` (`src/convert.zig`), and `DecodingReader` and
`EncodingWriter` (`src/io.zig`). Intel(R) Xeon(R) Processor @ 2.60GHz
(Ice Lake-SP, 4 vCPUs, AVX-512), in an orb, pinned to CPU 3 with
`taskset`. Zig 0.16.0, x86_64-linux, ReleaseFast for the host CPU. Branch
`wave4-io`, code at `cce58be`. Programs: `bench/io/` (`just bench-io`,
`just bench-io-sizes`); inputs: the `web` shape of `just bench-corpus`.

## Headline

- **Per call, the conveniences cost nothing measurable**: 1.0004x the
  direct call's time geomean over 220 rows, 1.0024x at 16 bytes; the worst
  row is +4.1% (0.6 ns), the best -5.8%.
- **The adapters keep up with a loop of direct calls**: `DecodingReader`
  is 0-2.1% slower on windows-1252, Shift_JIS, and gb18030 and 5.2-5.3%
  slower on UTF-8 (12 GB/s); `EncodingWriter` fed whole buffers is 0-1.5%
  slower on those and 3.2-5.8% on UTF-8. Fed 64-byte writes it is 2.8-5.3%
  slower on CJK and 30-105% on windows-1252 and UTF-8, mostly
  `std.Io.Writer`'s own cost of buffering 64-byte writes (74 us per MiB
  with no encoding at all, below).
- **Size**: a one-encoding convenience links what calling its
  `DecoderFor`/`EncoderFor` directly links, the same .rodata and up to
  101 bytes less .text. `DecodingReader` adds 1,345 bytes of .text over a
  hand loop of decode calls, and `EncodingWriter` 1,106 bytes, in
  ReleaseSmall.

## 1. Per call: convenience against direct

`bench-io percall`: every row times two functions, each called out of line
with its input escaping, as bench/zig/arm.zig does:

- `direct`: `var d: D = .init(e, .{ .bom = .keep }); d.decodeToUtf8(src, dst, true)`,
  and likewise for UTF-16 and the encoders.
- `convert`: `encoding.decodeToUtf8(D, e, src, dst, .{ .bom = .keep })`.

`runtime` rows use `Decoder` and `Encoder` with the encoding at run time;
`one` rows use `DecoderFor(&.{e})` and `EncoderFor(&.{e})`. Six
encodings (windows-1252, Shift_JIS, gb18030, EUC-KR, UTF-8, UTF-16LE,
decode only), four operations, five sizes from 16 bytes to 64 KiB: 220
rows. Each round times both arms of a row back to back, in an order that
alternates from row to row and round to round; 12 rounds of 20 ms samples.

Geomean of convert / direct, medians per row:

| rows    |   n | geomean |   min |   max |
| ------- | --: | ------: | ----: | ----: |
| 16 B    |  44 |  1.0024 | 0.987 | 1.023 |
| 64 B    |  44 |  1.0000 | 0.942 | 1.041 |
| 256 B   |  44 |  1.0000 | 0.986 | 1.019 |
| 4 KiB   |  44 |  0.9993 | 0.985 | 1.009 |
| 64 KiB  |  44 |  1.0001 | 0.989 | 1.018 |
| runtime | 110 |  1.0014 | 0.985 | 1.041 |
| one     | 110 |  0.9993 | 0.942 | 1.019 |
| all     | 220 |  1.0004 | 0.942 | 1.041 |

The convenience is the same `init` and one call with `last` set, plus a
switch on the status; LLVM inlines both into the caller. The rows that
come out slower are runtime decodes to UTF-16 at 16 and 64 bytes, +2.1% to
+4.1% (0.3 to 0.6 ns), where the call's prologue moves; the `one` rows of
the same decodes are even. A first run of the same rows, before the
results here, gave 1.0008x geomean and the same rows on top.

## 2. Throughput: adapters against a loop

`bench-io adapters`, 12 rounds:

- Decode, `direct`: a loop of `Decoder.decodeToUtf8(src[i..], out[0..4096], true)`
  over the whole input. `adapter`: `DecodingReader(Decoder)` over
  `std.Io.Reader.fixed(input)`, `streamRemaining` into a
  `std.Io.Writer.Discarding` with the same 4 KiB buffer.
- Encode, `direct`: the same loop with `Encoder.encodeFromUtf8`.
  `adapter`: `EncodingWriter(Encoder)` with a 4 KiB buffer onto a
  `Discarding` with a 4 KiB buffer, `writeAll` of the whole input, then
  `end`. `adapter64`: the same, written 64 bytes at a time.

In GB/s: the reader decodes UTF-8 at 11.8 GB/s against the loop's 12.5,
and windows-1252 at 2.61 against 2.63 (1 MiB; 3.79 against 3.88 at
64 KiB). A first run of 20 rounds, before these, gave the same picture:
reader +0.5-2.0% and +4.6-5.8% on UTF-8, writer +0-3.2%.

Findings:

1. **The reader decodes into the destination writer's buffer.** `stream`
   asks the writer for room with `writableSliceGreedy(4)` and decodes
   straight into it, so `streamRemaining` copies nothing. Asking for one
   byte instead cost 13-21% on UTF-8: when a character did not fit at the
   end of the 4 KiB buffer, the next call got the one-to-three-byte sliver
   and needed a decode call of its own for it. Filling the sliver in the
   same call cost the same, since the extra decode call is the cost. Asking
   for four drains the sliver instead. The price: a `Writer.fixed` with one
   to three bytes left fails a `stream` call with `error.WriteFailed`
   (documented; reads into slices take any length).
2. **What is left on UTF-8**, about 5%, is per 4 KiB chunk: the vtable
   calls, the `fillMore` check, and `Discarding`'s drain, about 17 ns
   against 330 ns of decoding. The loop also inlines the decoder into a single
   caller (comptime.md); a loop that calls a `noinline` step with
   `last = false` ran at 11.2 GB/s in a side run, below the reader. The
   legacy encodings, at 0.5 to 4 GB/s, do not see it.
3. **The writer batches small writes.** `drain` encodes what is buffered,
   then puts a write that fits in the emptied buffer instead of encoding it
   alone; before that change, 64-byte writes paid a second encode call per
   4 KiB (windows-1252 64 KiB: 2.82 to 3.50 GB/s). Writes larger than the
   buffer are encoded in place without a copy.
4. **64-byte writes cost what `std.Io.Writer` buffering costs.** A control
   that writes 1 MiB in 64-byte `writeAll`s through a 4 KiB buffer to a
   `Discarding`, with no encoding, takes 74 us (best of 200, pinned): the
   copy and the call per write. The UTF-8 writer's extra over whole writes
   is 92 us and windows-1252's 113 us: that copy, plus encoding from the
   buffer rather than from the caller's bytes.

The control, run as `zig build-exe -OReleaseFast` from a scratch file:

```zig
fn run(src: []const u8, out: []u8) u64 {
    var sink: std.Io.Writer.Discarding = .init(out);
    var buf: [4096]u8 = undefined;
    var w: std.Io.Writer = .{ .vtable = &.{ .drain = drain }, .buffer = &buf };
    sinkp = &sink.writer;
    var i: usize = 0;
    while (i < src.len) : (i += 64) w.writeAll(src[i..@min(src.len, i + 64)]) catch unreachable;
    w.flush() catch unreachable;
    return sink.fullCount();
}
// drain: writeAll the buffer to sinkp, then writeSplat a write that fits.
```


## 3. Size

`just bench-io-sizes` builds one program per way of calling (`bench/io/size.zig`).
Each reads stdin, converts it, and writes stdout; `none` only copies.
`direct_*` call the streaming type once over the whole input, `convert_*`
call the convenience over the same type, `stream_*` loop over 4 KiB chunks
by hand, and `reader_*`/`writer_*` use the adapter. Native target
(AVX-512), so the single-byte decoder is its AVX-512 build; compare within
a pair.

ReleaseSmall, in bytes, net of `none`:

| program            | type                                    | +rodata |  +text |
| ------------------ | --------------------------------------- | ------: | -----: |
| `direct_1252`      | `DecoderFor(&.{.windows_1252})`         |   1,055 |  4,579 |
| `convert_1252`     | the same, `decodeToUtf8`                |   1,055 |  4,478 |
| `direct_sjis`      | `DecoderFor(&.{.shift_jis})`            |  16,520 |  2,296 |
| `convert_sjis`     | the same, `decodeToUtf8`                |  16,520 |  2,284 |
| `direct_enc_sjis`  | `EncoderFor(&.{.shift_jis})`            |  36,800 |  1,994 |
| `convert_enc_sjis` | the same, `encodeFromUtf8`              |  36,800 |  1,986 |
| `direct_label`     | `Decoder`, encoding from argv           | 186,154 | 34,289 |

A convenience over a one-encoding type links that encoding and nothing
else, because the type is its first parameter: the convenience is
instantiated per type and calls only that type's `init` and decode. The
reader and writer add 128 and 32 bytes of .rodata, their vtables.
