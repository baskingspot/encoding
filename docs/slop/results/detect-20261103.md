# Detection: model, accuracy, size, and speed (decision 5)

2026-11-03, on an Intel Xeon Processor @ 2.60GHz (4 cores, an orb), Zig
0.16.0 ReleaseFast, branch `wave3-detect` on `origin/main` 3422e84.
Throughput and per-call times are pinned to core 3 with `taskset`.

## Headline

On 60 810 windows of 16 to 4096 bytes and 1 870 whole articles in 25
encodings and 56 languages, held out from training:

|                  | mean accuracy, 16 B | 32 B      | 64 B      | 128 B     | article   | tables  | whole articles | a 32-byte call |
| ---------------- | ------------------- | --------- | --------- | --------- | --------- | ------- | -------------- | -------------- |
| this package     | **92.0%**           | **95.3%** | **97.4%** | **98.7%** | **99.7%** | 32.3 KB | **61.2 MB/s**  | **5.7 µs**     |
| chardetng 0.1.17 | 88.9%               | 93.0%     | 94.2%     | 95.9%     | 98.6%     | 29.3 KB | 9.8 MB/s       | 6.6 µs         |
| uchardet 0.0.8   | 81.1%               | 91.4%     | 93.6%     | 94.2%     | 92.5%     | ~136 KB | 5.1 MB/s       | 10.7 µs        |

The means weigh each encoding alike. This package is behind chardetng on
windows-1252 below 64 bytes (91% against 96% at 16 bytes, 94% against 96%
at 32), on windows-1250 and ISO-8859-2 below 256 bytes (75% and 74%
against 89% and 90% at 16 bytes), on ISO-8859-4 at 16 bytes (59% against
67%), on ISO-8859-7 from 256 bytes up (95% against 100% at 1024 bytes and
on whole articles: it reads ISO-8859-7 as windows-1253, which is right
unless the text holds one of the few bytes they decode differently, such
as Ά), and by a point on a few other cells. It is ahead everywhere else,
most on the Baltic encodings (windows-1257 at 256 bytes: 99% against
81%), Turkish, visual Hebrew, and ISO-8859-6.

uchardet's numbers count its "UHC" as EUC-KR and "MAC-CYRILLIC" as
x-mac-cyrillic, and every name outside the standard ("ASCII", "IBM852",
"MAC-CENTRALEUROPE", "VISCII", an empty answer) as windows-1252, a
browser's fallback. Its KOI8-U column is about 67% because it answers
KOI8-R, which decodes Ukrainian's і, ї, є, and ґ differently.

## Decision 5: the model

Every arm below is the same detector and the same decoders with other
tables (`detect-train variant NAME`), on the same samples. Tables are the
bytes of model data the detector reads, counted from the generated
tables: single-byte pair costs, per-language class costs, the 256-byte
class map of each single-byte encoding, and the CJK models.

| arm                 | model                                                                          | tables               | 16 B | 32 B | 64 B | 128 B | article | windows-1252 at 16 / 32 / article | whole articles | a 32-byte call |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------- | ---- | ---- | ---- | ----- | ------- | --------------------------------- | -------------- | -------------- |
| **hybrid** (chosen) | class pairs per group, plus each language's class frequencies, languages mixed | 32 256               | 92.0 | 95.3 | 97.4 | 98.7  | 99.7    | 91 / 94 / 99                      | 61.2 MB/s      | 5.7 µs         |
| pairs               | class pairs per group only, chardetng's shape                                  | 32 256 (29 949 used) | 91.3 | 94.2 | 96.7 | 98.1  | 99.6    | 91 / 93 / 95                      | 61.7 MB/s      | 4.3 µs         |
| unigram             | each language's class frequencies only: the simple frequency model             | 13 758               | 83.2 | 90.7 | 94.6 | 97.2  | 99.5    | 80 / 85 / 97                      | 172.1 MB/s     | 4.9 µs         |
| max                 | pairs, a group's languages merged by the most favorable, as chardetng merges   | 32 256               | 89.6 | 93.2 | 95.9 | 97.3  | 99.2    | 91 / 93 / 91                      |                |                |

Verdict: **byte-pair scores win, and a frequency model alone is not
enough**. The unigram model, which is what a simple frequency detector is,
is 9 points behind at 16 bytes and 5 at 32, worst where encodings share a
script and differ by which letters sit where (Big5 at 16 bytes: 67%
against 99%; Shift_JIS 85% against 100%). It is three times faster per
byte and 18 KB smaller, but short input is what detection is for.

chardetng's shape, pair scores per group with the languages merged by the
most favorable, is the `max` arm: 2.4 points behind at 16 bytes and 0.5 on
whole articles, where a group's most permissive language excuses text of
another (Spanish read as Hungarian). Pricing each
language's own letters on top of the group's pairs and adding the
languages' probabilities (hybrid) fixes that. `joint` has a better mean
but loses windows-1252, the commonest legacy encoding, by 9 points at 16
bytes, so it is not taken. Per-language pair tables (split-latin) cost 14
KB for nothing.

On size, 512 listed characters per CJK model and 48 classes per group cost
0.2 points at 16 bytes and nothing from 64 bytes up against 1024 and 64,
and save 11.9 KB (44 140 to 32 256 bytes), so they are the defaults. That
leaves the tables 10% larger than chardetng's 29 344 bytes
(`chardetng::data::DETECTOR_DATA`, 28 224, and `SINGLE_BYTE_DATA`, 1 120,
by `nm -S` on the chardetng arm). Both detectors also use their decoders'
CJK indexes, which a program that decodes those encodings carries anyway.
uchardet's library has 136 122 bytes of `.rodata` and 9 624 of
`.data.rel.ro`.

The detector's state is 864 bytes and it allocates nothing.

## Where the time goes

On a 32-byte window, `init` takes about 0.15 µs, `feed` 1.4 µs, and
`guess` about 3.5 µs: per candidate it counts classes from the bytes seen,
prices each of its languages, and adds their probabilities with `exp2` and
`log2`. Moving the per-encoding class counts out of `feed` into `guess`
(counting raw bytes once in `feed`) took a call from 9.3 µs to 5.7 µs and
the state from 5.5 KB to 864 bytes. `guess` is the next thing to make
cheaper if short-input cost matters more.

## What the detector picks among

chardetng's set: UTF-8, Shift_JIS, EUC-JP, ISO-2022-JP, EUC-KR, Big5,
GBK, windows-874, windows-1250 to windows-1258, ISO-8859-2, -4, -5, -6,
-7, -8 (visual Hebrew), and -13, KOI8-U, and IBM866. These are the
encodings unlabeled legacy web content was written in. Left out:

- UTF-16LE and UTF-16BE, found by their byte order mark
  (`Encoding.forBom`) before detection, as browsers do.
- KOI8-R, ISO-8859-8-I, gb18030, ISO-8859-1 and -15 decode the same as
  KOI8-U, windows-1255, GBK, and windows-1252 but for a few symbols, so a
  guess of the latter decodes them.
- ISO-8859-3, -10, -14, -16, macintosh, and x-mac-cyrillic were rare on
  the web; adding them takes accuracy from the encodings they resemble.
- replacement and x-user-defined are not text encodings to guess.

## Method

`test/detect/README.md` describes the corpus, the trainer, and the
scoring. The corpus is 3 360 Wikipedia articles at pinned revisions; two
of every three train, the third tests. Test text is encoded with
encoding_rs into every encoding of its language's group. A guess is right
when it decodes the sample to the same text as the right encoding. Every
arm guesses each cut with a fresh detector, no top-level domain, and UTF-8
allowed. The training text is Wikipedia prose, so the accuracy is that of
encyclopedic prose; real pages add markup, which every candidate sees as
ASCII and prices alike.

Commands: `just detect-score` (accuracy and speed of the three arms),
`just detect-variants` (the table above). The speed rounds below ran the
arms back to back, five times, with `BENCH_MS=1500`; the table gives the
medians.
